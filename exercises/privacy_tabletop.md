# Privacy Governance Tabletop — Fictional Exercise

This exercise is fictional and discussion-only. It requires no access to real personal data, systems, accounts, or devices.

## Scenario

A fictional organization discovers that several internal teams interpret retention and deletion rules differently. A scheduled review also finds that some data-transfer records and access-review evidence are older than the organization's preferred assurance window.

## Objectives

- Clarify decision ownership for retention, deletion, access review, and transfer assurance.
- Practice prioritizing evidence gaps without exposing personal data.
- Discuss privacy-by-design, minimization, and proportionality.
- Identify which decisions require legal/privacy specialist review.
- Define measurable closure criteria for fictional control gaps.

## Discussion prompts

1. Which evidence should be reviewed first and why?
2. How should the team separate data-minimization questions from retention questions?
3. What information is necessary for executive reporting without exposing personal details?
4. What documentation should exist before changing a retention policy?
5. How should exceptions be time-bounded and re-evaluated?
6. What evidence would demonstrate that deletion controls operate as intended?
7. How can teams reduce collection of unnecessary data?

## Outputs

- Fictional action register.
- Evidence-refresh plan.
- Privacy decision log.
- Exception review schedule.
- Executive summary containing no personal data.

## Safety boundary

Use synthetic examples only. Do not collect, expose, deanonymize, track, or attempt to access real personal data for this exercise.
