# Incident Coordination Tabletop — Fictional Major Security Event

## Scenario

A fictional organization receives multiple defensive alerts suggesting that a business service may have experienced a security event. The technical cause is intentionally unspecified. Participants focus on coordination, evidence preservation, communications, privacy, and recovery decisions rather than investigation techniques.

## Objectives

- assign incident-command and functional roles;
- establish a shared incident timeline;
- preserve authorized evidence and decision records;
- coordinate legal/privacy/business communications;
- define containment decision rights at a governance level;
- verify service recovery and follow-up ownership.

## Starting information

Use `resources/incident_observation_template.md`, `resources/evidence_log_template.md`, and fictional records from the repository datasets.

## Discussion injects

1. Alerts from two monitoring sources disagree about the event start time.
2. A business owner asks whether the service must be taken offline.
3. A customer-facing team requests a public statement before facts are confirmed.
4. An analyst discovers that some expected telemetry is unavailable.
5. A recovery option is available, but the dependency map is incomplete.
6. The service appears healthy again, but open questions remain about evidence completeness.

## Questions

- Who can authorize high-impact containment actions?
- What evidence should be preserved before major changes are made?
- How should uncertainty be communicated to leadership?
- What privacy or legal stakeholders should be involved?
- What criteria must be met before declaring recovery complete?
- Which unanswered questions should move into post-incident follow-up rather than delay restoration?

## Outputs

Produce a fictional incident timeline, decision log, stakeholder map, evidence checklist, recovery-verification checklist, and lessons-learned backlog.

## Safety boundary

This is a discussion-only exercise. It does not provide instructions for accessing accounts, collecting data from third parties, disabling systems, erasing evidence, tracking people, bypassing protections, or conducting offensive activity.
